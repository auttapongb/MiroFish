# Scenario — Chiang Mai Flood & Evacuation Crisis

## Background
Chiang Mai, northern Thailand: the Ping River runs through a metro area of ~1.2 million people.
Monsoon season brings annual flood risk to riverside communities, Warorot Market, and the Old
City. A slow-moving tropical storm has dumped record rainfall across the upper Ping watershed,
swelling reservoirs and forcing dam operators to release water downstream.

## The crisis
- The **Mae Ngat and Mae Taeng dams** are at capacity; water must be released, but downstream
  riverside communities say they were **not warned in time**.
- The **Ping River has breached its banks** in three districts; evacuation orders cover
  riverside slums, the Night Bazaar, and low-lying villages.
- **Warorot Market** traders refuse to abandon their stalls and decades-old shops.
- Some **elderly residents refuse evacuation**, unwilling to leave homes and livestock.
- **Tourist areas** are emptying; hoteliers fear further damage to an already weak season.

## The tension
- **Who is responsible** for the late warning — dam operators, the irrigation department, or
  local government — is disputed and escalating.
- Residents are **split**: evacuate now vs. stay and protect property.
- **Upstream farmers** blame the dams for releasing too late; **downstream communities** blame
  them for releasing too much at once.
- Relief supplies and **volunteer networks** are mobilizing, but coordination is chaotic.

## What to simulate
> Simulate the next 14 days as the flood peaks and recedes. Who evacuates, who stays, who
> coordinates the response? How does blame over the dam release shape the aftermath — and which
> communities recover first?
