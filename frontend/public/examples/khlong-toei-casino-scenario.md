# Scenario — Khlong Toei Casino Development

## Background
Khlong Toei district, Bangkok: ~104,000 residents over 12.565 sq km, anchored by Bangkok Port —
the Port Authority of Thailand's container port on the Chao Phraya, moving ~1.26–1.5 million TEU
a year across 8 berths.

In July 2026 the government ordered a study to move **all** Bangkok Port cargo operations to
Laem Chabang (~130 km south-east). This unlocks **2,353 rai** of prime central-Bangkok land.

## The new plan — a casino
Breaking from the earlier "casino-free entertainment complex" proposal, the government announces
it will turn the port site into **Thailand's first legalized casino-entertainment complex**: a
full gambling casino (table games, slots, sports betting) alongside hotels, a convention centre,
retail, and luxury residences.

- **No dedicated on-site housing** for the port's informal settlement — the earlier 500–600 rai
  housing promise is dropped from the casino plan.
- The casino is framed as a **tourism and tax-revenue engine**, promoted as "world-class
  entertainment" while the gambling floor is the real draw.
- A **vending mandate** reserves new market stalls for "poor Thais", excluding migrant vendors.

## The tension
- Gambling has been **illegal in Thailand** for generations; casino legalization is controversial
  on moral, religious, and social grounds.
- Residents have heard relocation promises before: attempted **more than ten times since 2002**,
  never succeeded.
- The **community is split** between those who see casino jobs/revenue and those who fear
  gambling addiction, crime, and displacement.
- **Migrant workers** (~6–7% of residents) are **not counted** in the 12,600-household register
  and would be left with nothing — no housing, no stall rights.
- Religious leaders and elder residents **oppose gambling on moral grounds**.
- Some younger residents and small business owners see the casino as **new customers and jobs**.

## What to simulate
> Simulate the next 12 months as the casino development is announced. Who supports the casino,
> who resists, who negotiates? How does the community split between those chasing casino
> opportunity and those fighting displacement and gambling — and where do the 12,600 settlement
> households end up?
